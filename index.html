    Spore

    A browser inside the browser. Sites live in torrents, so reading one helps host it. No server and no account — your files go to readers, not to a host.

    Visit a site

    Paste a magnet link or an infohash somebody shared with you. It is fetched from whoever has it, not from a web host.

    Publish your own

    A folder, a .zip of one, or a page and its files. Relative links, and an index.html if you have one. It is hashed here in your browser, and this tab becomes its first seed.

    You will be asked whether to sign it before it goes out. Signing is what lets you release a new version later, and lets readers check it really came from you.

      Who published this

      Your name for this key, stored only in this browser and never published. Two people can call themselves the same thing; the key is the part that cannot be copied. Naming one you have reason to trust is how you tell them apart later.

      A signature proves the key, not the person. It shows this site was published by whoever holds that key, and that it has not been altered since. It says nothing about who they are, and the name= a site declares is their own claim about themselves.

      This browser cannot isolate sites the usual way

      Spore normally shows a site inside a sandboxed frame. This browser refuses to serve one: the page would arrive from the network instead of from the swarm, which is why nothing renders. Every browser on iOS behaves this way, because they all use the same engine.

      Sites can still be shown, without that frame. Most of the protection does not come from it:

      Scripts
      still impossible, and the per-site switch is withdrawn here
      Images, fonts, requests
      still confined to the site's own torrent
      A site navigating itself away
      still blocked
      Reading another site's files
      still refused
      A link you click
      can open an outside tab, and that site sees your IP address

      That last line is the whole difference, and it needs a deliberate click. Nothing happens on its own. Spore does not hide your IP from the peers you download from either, so this is a smaller change than it sounds — but it is a real one, and it is yours to make.
